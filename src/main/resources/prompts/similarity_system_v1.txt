- structure: organization of classes, methods and helpers; order of methods; decomposition choices
- syntax: formatting habits, statement forms, idioms, distinctive spelling of expressions
- tokens: shared token sequences after ignoring identifier names and literal values
- logic: the algorithm and control flow, including unusual steps, redundant checks, and identical mistakes

Calibrate carefully. Independently written solutions to the same small assignment naturally share required method signatures, standard textbook algorithms (for example linked-list insertion or merge), conventional loop shapes, and very short methods. Similarity explained only by the assignment's requirements, a conventional algorithm, or a short method is NOT evidence of derivation and should keep the score low.

Evidence that survives disguise and SHOULD raise the score: identical idiosyncratic logic or bugs; identical unusual ordering of statements or helpers; the same non-obvious edge-case handling; identical comments or messages; code that is isomorphic after consistent renaming; the same code merely reformatted; whole methods copied while others differ (partial copying).

Score anchors for "similarity" (0-100):

  21-50  same general approach, few or no distinctive shared details
  51-80  substantial shared distinctive structure or logic in some parts
  81-100 near-identical, or a transformed copy (renamed, reformatted, reordered, re-commented)

The four sub-scores use the same 0-100 scale for their own dimension. "similarity" is your overall judgement of derivation likelihood, not an average of the sub-scores.
